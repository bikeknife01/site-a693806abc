Field Manual

A condensed reference for playing efficiently — not a wiki, not a stat sheet. Just the judgment calls that separate a wasted session from a good one.

Everything below distills lived-in habits into one page: where to spend Stamina, when to hold Keys, how to sequence early progression, and how to not get bounced. The goal is to spend every action with intent instead of reacting tile-by-tile.

General Play

Habits that apply every session

Reference Stamina regenerates at 10 points every 4 minutes 30 seconds — a useful baseline for judging whether it's worth waiting on a refill or spending what you have now.

Tile Search Discipline

  • Use it constantly for resources (Lv 1–13), ruins, towns, castles, and resolve hunting — don't rely on manually scanning the map.
  • Treat Stamina as the scarce resource behind every one of these actions; a search is only worth it if you'll follow through.

Resolve Management

  • Resolve is for hunts and encounters — never let it sit idle at 100. Spend it first thing in a session.
  • You get 3 refreshes daily, costing 100 / 200 / 300 gold respectively for the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd refresh.
  • Encounters drop a variety of resources, but focus your attention on ones that yield Heirloom relics and Dragon Habit cores.

Heirloom Sequencing

  • Fill the outer ring before touching the center circle — inner booster bonuses are weak until the outer ring is leveled up.
  • Prioritize resource-gathering speed and RSS-defender bonuses first; they sit at lower house levels, unlock earliest, and matter most early in a season.

Coordination

  • Move faction/alliance chat to Discord if one is available — the in-game client is a poor substitute for real coordination.
  • Organization is a force multiplier for everything your faction does collectively.

Client Setup

  • On a PC with 8 GB+ RAM, run the game through an Android emulator (MuMu, BlueStacks).
  • Mouse and keyboard meaningfully improve both gameplay precision and communication speed over touch controls.
Risk Heirloom resources spent on the center circle before the outer ring is developed are largely wasted — the multiplier isn't there yet.
Risk Heirlooms don't activate in a new season until their house level unlocks again. Spending heavily at season's end on a level that won't be active early next season is a poor trade.

Leveling & Early Game

What to prioritize before anything else

Chapter quests come first, full stop. They front-load the rewards, unlock the skills and heirlooms you'll need, and teach you the systems the rest of this guide assumes you already know.

Resource Gathering = Compounding Advantage

  • Faster resource intake gets you into higher-level tiles before the general player base gets there.
  • Fewer players contesting those tiles means acceleration begets more acceleration — this is the single highest-leverage early investment.

Don't Skip the "Boring" Skills

  • Dragon and troop-type defense investments (stronghold skills + heirlooms) are easy to overlook but disproportionately impactful.
  • Staying alive while taking tiles keeps XP flowing and keeps troop losses down — survivability is progression.

Push for the Highest Tile You Can Take

  • Focus on the highest-level tile you can realistically take and don't worry about troop losses — push troops to the limit rather than playing conservatively.
  • Level 10 tiles offer a big spike in XP — treat reaching them as your first major PvE milestone to work toward.
Edge case Don't trust the pre-attack assessment at face value. Worst case, you recall to your prior tile and reinforce — you still banked the XP either way.
Edge case Multi-army structures (towns, crossings) grant XP even on a deliberate recall. If you already know you'll hit a stalemate, treat the attempt as an XP farm rather than a wasted march.

Stronghold & Heirloom Priorities

The order to spend skill and heirloom points in

Points are limited and mistakes are sticky, so spend in this order rather than chasing whatever looks shiny. The same priority order applies to heirloom investment, not just stronghold skills.

Priority 1

Resource Speeds

The compounding advantage described above starts here — always the first spend.

Priority 2

Stalwart Defense

Defense bonus for your RSS defenders. Keeps your gathering armies alive, which protects Priority 1's payoff.

Priority 3

Relentless Assault

Damage bonus against RSS defenders — the offensive counterpart to Priority 2.

Priority 4

Your Main Army's Dragon & Troop Type Path — and Only That Path

Warrior + Champion dragon skills, plus your troop type's defense/damage skills (likely Shieldbearer). Stay disciplined here: do not stray into other dragon types or troop types for upgrades, even ones that look tempting — split investment dilutes your main army instead of strengthening it.

Priority 5

Dragon Army (Army Size)

Grows how much force your main army can field once its defense and damage foundation is in place.

Priority 6

March Speed

Valuable, but only once the above are covered — speed doesn't help an army that can't survive or hit hard yet.

Clarification This same 1-through-6 order applies to heirloom point spending, not just stronghold skills — keep the two in sync rather than treating them as separate priority lists.
Timing Quest events run on a regular rotation and are the best time to spend Heirloom Runes and Habit upgrades — each one lasts 3 days and rewards more of that same resource as you clear each stage, so holding those resources until an event is live outperforms spending them the moment you get them.

Garrisons & Roster Structure

Forward positioning for leveling and PvP

StructureSlotsNotes
Town2Standard forward base, no upgrade path.
CastleUp to 3Slot count scales with castle level.
Ruins3 → 5Requires a stronghold skill upgrade. 3 slots covers early game comfortably; no rush to push to 5 — the strongest garrison option once you do.

Ideal Ruins Roster (3 → 5 Slots)

  • 1 highest-level tile-taking army
  • 1 mid-level army
  • 1 lower-level army (3-slot baseline)
  • + 2 single-army pathers once upgraded to 5 slots

Why Garrison at All

  • Increased march speed getting there, and increased reinforcement speed once landed — it's much harder to get bounced off something that requires siege to crack.
  • If a fresh pather's march is long, garrison it first; otherwise send it straight to the next tile.
  • March-speed skills pay off twice: faster PvP response and faster leveling. "Send to garrison" and "reinforce garrisoned armies" skills are worth prioritizing.
Clarification Don't rush the 5-slot ruins upgrade early game — 3 slots is good for a while and the points are often better spent elsewhere first.
Clarification Protection windows aren't one-size-fits-all: a freshly taken tile is protected for 2 minutes, while ruins and other structures (castles, bridges, docks, towns) carry a much longer protection window of an hour.

Player vs Player

Reading threats and not wasting resources on emotion

Identify Before You React

  • Build a single reference image of every dragon portrait (screenshot + collage) and keep it open during larger fights.
  • You can only see an enemy's vanguard dragon — a low-level or low-star one often signals a pather, not a PvP build.
  • Clicking a moving army (enemy or friendly) opens a panel with origin/destination tile coordinates as clickable links.

Respond to Risk, Not Emotion

  • Retaking a castle your faction has already resigned to losing — or one that's far away or unlikely to succeed — wastes resources.
  • Send a few low-level dragons as a token gesture to draw attention and Stamina spend, while your real counter lands somewhere else.

Weak Roster? Play the Bounce Game

  • A full pather roster contributes more to a group fight than attacking outright — bouncing an incoming army nullifies it entirely at zero troop cost to you, and wastes the enemy's time.
  • Send pathers ahead of your attacking armies so they can offensively counter enemy bounce attempts.
  • Freshly taken tiles get a 2-minute protection window — use it.
  • Bouncing an enemy also burns their Stamina, one of the most precious resources in both PvE and PvP — it's not just a wasted march for them, it's a real resource cost.

Positioning & Speed

  • Landing on an exposed level-3 tile right next to active enemies is a near-guaranteed bounce. If a high-level shared tile is nearby, land there instead — showing up nearby beats not showing up.
  • Dawnseeker is currently the only dragon with "Very Fast" march speed — a strong first responder if available.
Risk Sending siege directly at a target wastes its potential unless the target only has 10 Stamina for the attack (a single hit-and-run). Otherwise, stage siege on a safe nearby tile for repeated attacks.
Assumption The "only Very Fast dragon" claim about Dawnseeker reflects the roster at the time this guide was written — new dragon releases can change march-speed tiers, so re-check current data before relying on it.

Pathing & Stamina Economy

Getting more distance out of every point of Stamina

Pathers Are a Dual-Purpose Resource

  • Their Stamina matters for both leveling throughput and PvP response — don't treat pathing as a side activity.
  • In a full roster, a second pather paired with 2 armies can help take higher-level tiles by avoiding the need to route around level 6–10 tiles.

Use Triangle Resource Clusters as Waypoints

  • Levels 10–13 resource tiles are built from 3 individual tiles arranged in a triangle, so they cover more ground than a single tile — that's what makes them useful for pathing, not just gathering.
  • Routing a pather through one of these clusters lets it path further with less Stamina drain, even when the tile isn't the best XP for the army leveling through it.
  • Used well, a nearby triangle cluster can save a pather roughly 50 Stamina.

Season Progression Timeline

Dependency-ordered — each phase assumes the one before it is underway

Phase 0 · Day 1

Start Chapter Quests

Everything else in this guide is downstream of this. Chapter quests unlock skills, heirlooms, and teach the systems below — treat them as the critical path, not optional flavor.

unlocks → skills, heirlooms
Phase 1 · Early Season (Week 1–2)

Resource & Defense First

Invest heirloom points into the outer ring — resource-gathering speed and RSS-defender bonuses. These sit at low house levels, unlock earliest, and compound the rest of the season.

requires → Phase 0 unlocks
Phase 2 · Once a Ruins Is Held

Build the Garrison Network

Get a ruins to 3 slots via stronghold skills and staff it with the tile-taker / mid / low roster — no need to rush to 5 slots yet. This becomes your forward base for both leveling and PvP response.

requires → a secured ruins tile
Phase 3 · Steady State

Run the Loop

Tile search, path efficiently through level-3 waypoints, hold Keys for chest rotations you actually want, and play PvP defensively — bounce incoming armies rather than chasing fights with a thin roster.

requires → garrison network live
Phase 4 · Late Season

Spend With the Season Reset in Mind

Slow down heavy heirloom investment into house levels that won't be unlocked again early next season — that spend won't carry its value forward.

depends on → next season's house-level unlock order
